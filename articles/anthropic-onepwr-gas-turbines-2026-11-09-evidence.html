Mechanism divergence — what is actually disputed

Revision 3 treated this as three mutually exclusive accounts. Checked against the sources, it is a two-against-one split with an outlier:

  • [2] DCD, subheading: "Takes over OnePWR order of Caterpillar gen-sets"; text: Anthropic will "partner with Texas-based equipment supplier OnePWR Solutions".
  • [3] CNBC: Anthropic "bought commitments for 1.7 gigawatts of heavy-duty generators in late September from OnePWR Solutions".
  • [4] TechCrunch: Anthropic "had planned to buy them from OnePWR but instead went directly to Caterpillar, the manufacturer".

Acquiring OnePWR's commitments and taking over its order are compatible descriptions; DCD and CNBC sit together. TechCrunch's direct contract with the manufacturer is the divergent account. No retrieved source explains the difference, and the FT could not be read. The article therefore states what every account supports — Anthropic acquired control of generators OnePWR had ordered — and declines to adjudicate who sold them. Body and this file now say the same thing; revision 3 had them contradicting each other.

Waitlist framing — reconciled

Revision 1 recorded the "six-year waitlist" phrase [5] and the two-year availability window [2] as an unreconcilable discrepancy. That was an error: [4] reports that "the backlog for natural gas turbines stretches into the early 2030s", which describes the queue for new orders, while [2] describes delivery of already-ordered equipment. Both claims can be true. Note that [5] was never read — the phrase is quoted from a headline, and the article now says so.
