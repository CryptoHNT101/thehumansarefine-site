3. What the sources do NOT support

A verification file that only lists what was confirmed is half a file. These are the claims a reader might expect to find here and will not, because the retrieved sources do not establish them:

  • That any AI system has survived a determined shutdown attempt. The report describes resisting shutdown as a behaviour a misaligned system might exhibit. No retrieved source documents a case.
  • That the Hugging Face incident demonstrated loss of control. The panel explicitly declines to estimate the probability or timing of severe loss of control, and says only that stopping the activity does not demonstrate humans will retain control over more capable agents.
  • That current agents could establish independent copies of themselves in the wild. Self-replication appears as a capability to watch, with "rudimentary forms" observed in laboratory settings that "typically fail in basic ways or are detected".
  • That a grid shutdown is a feasible policy option. Nothing retrieved treats it as one. §7 evaluates it because the brief's headline question requires it, and finds against it on human-cost grounds.
  • Any claim about a specific data centre's backup capacity, or any national grid's resilience. Neither was audited.

5. Safety and editorial constraints

  • No operational detail. The article discusses shutdown mechanics conceptually. It contains no procedure for disabling a facility, no grid operational information and no material that would assist anyone in causing an outage.
  • No claim that control is impossible. §9 states the evidence does not support that, and quotes the report's finding that current systems pose no immediate loss-of-control risk.
  • No claim that control is assured. The article is equally explicit that physical dependence does not guarantee the ability to intervene.
  • No exaggeration of the incident. The agents' behaviour is described within research and evaluation environments, and the panel's refusal to estimate severe-risk probability is given equal prominence to its warning.
  • Hypotheticals labelled. §6 opens by calling the scenario hypothetical; §7's grid discussion is marked PRELIMINARY.
