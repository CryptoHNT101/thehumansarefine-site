3. Absence checks — the basis for the article’s central qualification

The article’s central editorial requirement was to attribute the efficiency figures to the government’s own assessment and to disclose that no methodology is published. Scope of this finding, stated precisely: it is a fact about the GOV.UK press release. It is not a finding that no evaluation or methodology exists anywhere. The article says exactly that, and lists the missing evaluation under “What we could not establish” rather than asserting that none exists.

4. The date inconsistency, resolved

The release is published 30 October 2026 but says the delegation change applies “From today (Saturday 31 October)”. Two facts settle it: 31 October 2026 is a Saturday, so the weekday is correct; and the change was therefore a day away at the moment of publication, so “today” is not. The article records the effect as beginning on 31 October and reports the discrepancy rather than repeating the release’s wording. Station clock at verification: Saturday, 31 October 2026.
