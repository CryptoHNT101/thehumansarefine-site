Source-relationship note, material to how this story should be read. S1, S3 and S4 are the UK institute’s own account. S2 is Anthropic’s account, published three months later. The two organisations did this work together: Anthropic supplied unredacted transcripts to AISI, and the technical report states that both groups shared and discussed findings. They are therefore not two independent confirmations of one event, and the article says so in its own section rather than treating agreement as corroboration.

Extraction disclosure. Both PDFs were converted to text locally. The extraction introduced two known artefacts, both disclosed and handled by the verification harness: line-break hyphenation (“mon- itoring”) and straight double quotes where the PDF renders typographic ones. Quotations in the article differ from the PDF surface only in that normalisation.

Claim verification

A machine harness tested the article against all four captured source files after the final edit, not before it.

  • 86 assertions, 86 passing, 0 failing. Composition: 49 quotation checks, 28 figure and fact checks, five article-level claim tests and four heading checks.
  • Three checks pass only in a looser comparison mode, each labelled as such in the output because of the PDF layout artefacts described above. No check was weakened to make it pass.
  • Four negative controls were run against the harness itself: given a fabricated outcome, a fabricated quotation, a fabricated confirmation and a fabricated METR publication, it correctly refused to find each one.
  • The harness caught genuine defects in earlier drafts before human review did, including a quoted sentence that had been composed rather than copied. Those are recorded in the corrections log below.

Independent review

The article was reviewed in two rounds by a reviewing agent running on a different model family from the agent that drafted it.

  • Round one: FAIL, with ten numbered defects. Six were genuine and were fixed. Four quoted sentences that are not in the article and do not describe it; those four were void, and the reviewer confirmed all four in round two.
  • Round two: PASS WITH NOTES, with three wording notes. Two were applied; the third was already satisfied in the text.
  • The reviewer’s stated limitation is preserved here rather than omitted: it could not extract text from the two AISI PDFs and judged PDF-derived claims against extracts supplied by the drafting role. This review therefore validates the article’s characterisations of those documents; it does not independently re-read them.
  • Added after the review, and disclosed: the dated statement at C22, which says that AISI has announced no completed review. It is the same class of statement as C21, it was added to keep the article’s central open question current, and the reviewer did not see it. Any reader can repeat the check.

Known limitations of this Evidence File

  1. The reviewer never read the two AISI PDFs itself. The quotations are machine-verified against those files, but the interpretive judgement of them is single-model.
  2. AISI’s blog post could not be dated from the page, and no date is asserted for it.
  3. The 122-attempt figure and the 43-run figure are not reconciled in the sources, and are not reconciled here.
  4. Whether METR has since published a review is a statement about a search on 29 October 2026, not about the world.
  5. Quotations from the PDFs were extracted mechanically; hyphenation and quote style were normalised, and each such case is disclosed rather than silently smoothed.
