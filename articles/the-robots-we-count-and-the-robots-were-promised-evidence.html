EVIDENCE FILE

Evidence File - The Robots We Count and the Robots We're Promised

Every claim in the article resolves to a numbered source record below or to an explicitly declared gap. Sources were retrieved and read; where a source could not be retrieved, the gap is recorded rather than filled.

0. HOW TO READ THIS FILE

Every claim in the companion article is supported by a numbered source record here; the draft cites by document rather than by tag. The tags are:

Tag Meaning
[FACT] Established by a primary source I retrieved myself
[CORP] Corporate statement — a company or its executive asserting something about their own product or plans
[FCST] Forecast — a prediction about the future, attributed to whoever made it
[SEC] Reported by secondary press; I did not retrieve the underlying primary record
[GAP] Not retrieved. Named as a gap. Nothing is claimed

Rule applied throughout: a number I could not retrieve is a [GAP], not an estimate. Where the brief supplied a source I could not open, I say so and do not paraphrase what it probably says.

S7 — IFR definition of "industrial robot" — [FACT] RETRIEVED

  • Locator: https://ifr.org/industrial-robots
  • Retrieved: 2 Nov 2026, full page text
  • Verbatim: IFR's use of the term is based on ISO 8373:2021 — an "automatically controlled, reprogrammable multipurpose manipulator, programmable in three or more axes, which can be either fixed in place or fixed to a mobile platform for use in automation applications in an industrial environment."
  • Why this source exists: an earlier draft of this article described the five million as "the bolted-down arms that weld, paint and palletise," implying no humanoid could be among them. That was wrong, and an independent reviewer caught it. The category is defined by use environment, not by form factor, so a factory humanoid falls inside it. The article's central contrast survives, but it must be stated as a contrast of place, not of shape.
  • Also relevant: IFR's statistical department compiles installations of "multipurpose industrial robots" for around 40 countries, broken down by application, customer industry and robot type.

S8 — Acemoglu & Restrepo, Robots and Jobs — [FACT] RETRIEVED

  • Locator: https://www.nber.org/papers/w23285 — NBER Working Paper 23285, DOI 10.3386/w23285
  • Issue date: March 2017
  • Published version: Journal of Political Economy, 128(6), 2020, pp. 2188–2244 (per the NBER page's "Published Versions" field)
  • Retrieved: 2 Nov 2026 (research pass) and again 3 Nov 2026 (lead re-retrieval, full abstract text, content check passed)
  • Verbatim extract, the sentence the draft quotes:

"According to our estimates, one more robot per thousand workers reduces the employment to population ratio by about 0.18-0.34 percentage points and wages by 0.25-0.5 percent."

  • Match: exact, character for character, including the hyphenated ranges.
  • Scope, mandatory for the draft: the estimate concerns industrial robots in US local labour markets (commuting zones), 1990–2007. It is not evidence about humanoid robots and not evidence about generative AI. The abstract's own wording is "industrial robot usage between 1990 and 2007 on US local labor markets."
  • Provenance caveat: the quoted sentence was read on the 2017 working-paper abstract. The JPE version is paywalled from this station and was not opened, so this file does not assert that the published wording is identical. The draft cites JPE as the peer-reviewed home and NBER as the text read — that is the honest formulation, and it is why both appear in the article's parenthetical.
  • Why it matters: this is the strongest measured estimate in the package on automation and employment, and it is an estimate about a different technology. That tension is the section's point, not a weakness in it.

S9 — IMF Notes 2026/002, Global Economic and Financial Implications of Artificial Intelligence — [FACT] RETRIEVED

  • Locator: https://www.elibrary.imf.org/view/journals/068/2026/002/article-A001-en.xml
  • Full printed title: "Global Economic and Financial Implications of Artificial Intelligence: Lessons from a Scenario Planning Exercise"
  • Publication date: 3 April 2026 · Volume 2026, Issue 002 · DOI 10.5089/9798229042482.068.A001
  • Retrieved: 2 Nov 2026 (research pass) and again 3 Nov 2026 (lead re-retrieval; section heading "Fiscal Policy: Pressures on Revenue and Expenditure" located, string checks on the quoted text and on the qualification below both passed)
  • Verbatim extract, the passage the draft quotes:

"Erosion of the labor tax base will necessitate adaptation of tax systems. Declines in employment and wage income weaken personal income tax bases, particularly under rapid diffusion, even after accounting for potentially higher wages for remaining workers. Shifting taxation from labor to capital through higher corporate income taxes may only partially offset these losses given capital mobility, market concentration, and international tax competition."

  • Match: exact.
  • THE QUALIFICATION THE DRAFT MUST CARRY. The quoted sentences end mid-paragraph. The same paragraph continues:

"…Therefore, tax systems should rebalance toward better capturing economic rents while minimizing distortions on investment decisions, supported by a stronger AI-enabled tax administration. Avoiding blunt "robot taxes," strengthening capital income taxation at the individual level with safeguards against base erosion, and leveraging AI to improve compliance and enforcement are important across scenarios."

  • Verified independently in this entry's second retrieval: the strings "Avoiding blunt" and "robot taxes" both occur in the retrieved page text. So the source does not support an automation-levy reading; it explicitly advises against blunt robot taxes. Cutting the quotation at "international tax competition" is not a misquotation — the words are verbatim and the cut lands on a sentence boundary — but it suppresses the source's own opposite recommendation. The article must carry the qualification, and does.
  • Classification, mandatory: this is scenario analysis, not an empirical estimate and not a forecast with a point estimate. The note synthesises a closed-door workshop held 10–11 December 2025 with the Economics of Transformative AI Initiative (EconTAI) at the University of Virginia, with roughly 50 participants, and states that "In both scenarios, transformative AI technologies were assumed to attain the capacity to perform a wide range of cognitive and manual tasks by the early 2030s." Attribute as IMF staff scenario analysis. Never as measured data.
  • Access note: direct browser navigation to this URL hit a verification wall (HTTP 405) on the research pass; the body was returned by the fetch route. The second retrieval succeeded by the same route.

S11 — Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Article 55 — [FACT] RETRIEVED

  • Locators: official OJ text, EUR-Lex CELEX 32024R1689 (read in full, research pass); https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/55/ (read, research pass and again 3 Nov 2026)
  • Retrieved: 2 Nov 2026 and again 3 Nov 2026
  • The full Article 55(1) list, official text — all four sub-points:

"(a) perform model evaluation in accordance with standardised protocols and tools reflecting the state of the art, including conducting and documenting adversarial testing of the model with a view to identifying and mitigating systemic risks; (b) assess and mitigate possible systemic risks at Union level, including their sources, that may stem from the development, the placing on the market, or the use of general-purpose AI models with systemic risk; (c) keep track of, document, and report, without undue delay, to the AI Office and, as appropriate, to national competent authorities, relevant information about serious incidents and possible corrective measures to address them; (d) ensure an adequate level of cybersecurity protection for the general-purpose AI model with systemic risk and the physical infrastructure of the model."

  • Verified independently in this entry's second retrieval: the strings "perform model evaluation in accordance", "assess and mitigate possible systemic risks", "keep track of, document, and report" and "ensure an adequate level of cybersecurity" all occur on the retrieved page. All four sub-points exist.
  • Correction recorded here: an earlier revision of the draft listed only (a), (c) and (d), presenting them as the obligations of Article 55. That list was incomplete and is now fixed to include (b) — the mitigation duty, which is the only sub-point that requires a provider to mitigate systemic risk rather than evaluate, report or secure. (a) is evaluation, (c) is incident reporting, (d) is cybersecurity.
  • Rendering caveat: artificialintelligenceact.eu injects definition tooltips inline into the sentence flow, which corrupts a clean verbatim read of that page (it also displays proposed Digital Omnibus amendments marked "AMENDED"/"NEW"). The official OJ text is therefore the quotation authority; the website is the accessibility route. Both were read.
  • Application date — CONFIRMED: Article 113, second paragraph, point (b): "Chapter III Section 4, Chapter V, Chapter VII and Chapter XII and Article 78 shall apply from 2 August 2025, with the exception of Article 101." Article 55 sits in Chapter V, so the systemic-risk obligations applied from 2 August 2025. The draft's claim is correct. The Regulation otherwise applies generally from 2 August 2026; the article names the early Chapter V date, which is the right one for Article 55.

S12 — European Parliament, A8-0005/2017 and P8_TA(2017)0051 — [FACT] RETRIEVED

  • Locators: https://www.europarl.europa.eu/doceo/document/A-8-2017-0005_EN.html (committee report) and https://www.europarl.europa.eu/doceo/document/TA-8-2017-0051_EN.html (adopted resolution)
  • Retrieved: 2 Nov 2026, via the station browser; report page text 156,627 characters, adopted-resolution body 68,813 characters, both read in full

"…the likelihood of levying tax on the work performed by a robot or a fee for using and maintaining a robot should be examined in the context of funding the support and retraining of unemployed workers whose jobs have been reduced or eliminated;"

  • Half two — zero occurrences of "tax". TRUE.
