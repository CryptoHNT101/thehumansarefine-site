What this article builds on, and what it deliberately does not

  • The spine is ground-based financing: a lease, the notes that fund it, the amortisation, the counterparties, and the conditions still outstanding. The orbital question is reported as a named publication's reported claim and is not load-bearing.
  • The article does not state or imply that any lender restriction exists as a contractual term. No covenant detail appears, because no document retrieved describes one.
  • The article does not state or imply that AI caused anything. It is a corporate-finance story about the purchase and leasing of AI hardware.
  • The $40 billion figure and the $44 billion figure are reported as different measurements from different documents. No reconciliation is offered, because none is stated in the sources. The commentary block says the same thing and does not explain the gap either.
  • The 26 October announcement is dated in the body and in the standfirst. It is not presented as new. The later, genuinely current event is the 20 November pricing.

Revision record

This is revision r4 of the narrowed draft, authorised by the Commander after the earlier HOLD-PENDING-SOURCE. The hold arose because the original dispatch's spine rested on an outlet named but never retrieved. The hold is resolved by the SpaceX primary and the SEC filing carrying the financing spine, and by moving the unretrieved claim out of the headline and out of the load-bearing argument. The prior screen and the evidence re-check are retained unedited alongside this package.

Revision r1 went to independent review and returned REVISE. Four substantive findings were accepted and corrected: a commentary heading that asserted the unclosed lease in the present tense; a commentary sentence that explained the gap between the $44 billion and the $40 billion, which no document explains, and that miscast the $40 billion as the amount the lease repays rather than as expected aggregate principal payments; a present-tense meta description; and a publication date attributed to the Financial Times report which the paywalled page does not render. One procedural finding, that the reviewer could not read the package files untruncated in its review slot, was addressed by staging the round-2 review copies to a location the reviewer can read. The round-1 verdict is retained unedited in reviews/REVIEW-independent-spacex-r1-2026-11-22.json and must not be reused against this revision.

Revision r2 went to a second, different independent reviewer and also returned REVISE. Two substantive findings were accepted and corrected. First, the sentence "Neither release, and not the filing, says where the equipment will be" was a self-cancelling absence claim: it contradicted the quoted filing language two sentences later and the standfirst's own statement that the releases call the equipment part of the terrestrial compute buildout. It is replaced by the true and narrower claim that no document states a site, a country or a jurisdiction. Second, the clause "which is why the transaction had to be disclosed in this form" asserted a disclosure obligation the filing does not state and mischaracterised Item 8.01, which is headed Other Events; it is replaced by the two facts alone. The round-2 verdict is retained unedited in reviews/REVIEW-independent-spacex-r2-2026-11-22.json. Neither earlier verdict applies to this revision.

Revision r3 went to a third independent review and returned REVISE with one substantive finding, two bookkeeping findings and three informational notes. The substantive finding was that the closing paragraph claimed "no statement beyond the releases and the filing was retrieved" while the article quotes the Financial Times headline in an earlier paragraph; the sentence now says "no company statement", which is what it meant. The second accepted finding was that a sentence asserted other outlets had restated the FT without naming or retrieving any; it is replaced by the corroboration standard stated conditionally, so the rule stands and the unevidenced observation is gone. Both bookkeeping findings were corrected: the markdown source now labels its own revision correctly, and the disposition of round-2 finding R2-F4 is recorded in the revision log. Two informational notes were also taken: a two-sentence quotation was described as "a sentence", and the meta description described the $40 billion as the lease value rather than as expected principal payments. The round-3 verdict is retained unedited in reviews/REVIEW-independent-spacex-r3-2026-11-22.json. No earlier verdict applies to this revision.

Limitations recorded by the drafting desk

  • The Financial Times article body was not retrieved. Only its headline is quoted. The terms it describes are therefore unverified here.
  • The Financial Times report's publication date was not verified. The paywall page renders no date, so the article does not date the report.
  • No rating-agency document was retrieved. No rating appears in the article.
  • The lease rate is not public in any document reviewed. The article says so.
  • Whether the master finance lease has been entered into is not stated in any document reviewed. The article says so.
  • The difference between $44 billion of notes and $40 billion of expected principal is unexplained in the sources. The article says so rather than guessing.
  • No company was asked for comment. The article records that plainly and represents no response from anyone.
