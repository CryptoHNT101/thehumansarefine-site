Commission Opens the AI Act's Human-Rights Audit Form for Comment

The Article 27 fundamental rights impact assessment becomes an obligation on 2 December 2027. Before then, the AI Office wants to know whether the questionnaire it has drafted actually works.

The European Commission has opened public feedback on the questionnaire that some organisations will have to complete before they put high-risk artificial intelligence into service, and is recruiting volunteers to test the draft on their own systems.

In a news article published on 16 November 2026, the Commission said it is collecting comments on a draft template for the fundamental rights impact assessment required by Article 27 of the EU Artificial Intelligence Act. The article states the purpose directly: “The Commission is collecting feedback on the draft template for a questionnaire to facilitate certain deployers of high-risk AI system in complying with their obligations to prepare a fundamental rights impact assessment under Article 27 AI Act.”

The obligation falls on a defined group. Under Article 27, certain deployers of high-risk AI systems listed in Annex III of the Act must assess the impact their use of such a system may have on fundamental rights. The assessment has to be carried out before the system is deployed, and the information in the template has to be updated if, during use, the deployer considers that any of the elements listed in the first paragraph of Article 27 has changed or is no longer current. The Commission's article gives the date the duty bites: “Deployers will need to comply with this obligation as of 2 December 2027.”

The draft template was produced by the Commission's AI Office under Article 27(5) of the Act, with input from the EU Agency for Fundamental Rights. According to the article, it follows the structure of Article 27(1) and sets out questions intended to guide deployers through the assessment and simplify compliance. It is accompanied by a draft explanatory notice covering which deployers must conduct the assessment, when it must be conducted, and how the Article 27 duty relates to the obligations of the provider of the high-risk system.

Once the template is finalised, deployers will have to use it to prepare the assessment and notify the results to the market surveillance authority, as required by Article 27(3). The article adds that, once adopted, the template will also be set up as an automated tool on the AI Act Single Information Platform, so that deployers can fill it in there.

Two deadlines appear in the announcement. Public feedback on the draft template runs until 15 January 2027. Separately, deployers that will be subject to Article 27 are invited to express interest in testing the template by 4 December 2026, which is the earlier deadline of the two.

The testing exercise is described as a selection rather than an open door. The AI Office will support participants with an introductory session, answers to questions during the exercise and a closing session to discuss results. It may have to choose among the expressions of interest to secure what the article calls a balanced representation of areas of use, use cases, types and sizes of deployers, and Member States. Participation is voluntary and, the article says, without prejudice to obligations under the Act.

The consultation itself is hosted on the Commission's EUSurvey platform under the title Stakeholder consultation on the draft fundamental rights impact assessment under the AI Act (Article 27). That page was opened for this article and returned the survey shell, including a mandatory-field notice, but not the numbered questions.

What the announcement does not contain is as notable as what it does. It gives no text of the final template, no count of how many deployers fall within Article 27, no estimate of what compliance will cost, and no statement about enforcement beyond the Article 27(3) notification to market surveillance authorities. The draft explanatory notice and template are offered as a download from the same page; that document could not be retrieved for this article, so the substance of the questions it asks is not reported here.

The article also does not describe the penalties that attach to a failure to prepare or notify an assessment. It is a consultation notice: an invitation to comment on a form, and a call for volunteers to try it out, issued roughly thirteen months before the obligation is due to apply.

FOR HUMAN CONSUMPTION

Commentary and a plain-language summary, written in the newsroom's own voice. It is not reporting, it introduces no source, and it should not be quoted as a finding.

Some artificial intelligence systems are used in ways that can affect people's rights: deciding who gets a service, who gets checked more closely, who gets flagged. Under the EU's AI Act, certain organizations must assess the potential impact on fundamental rights before deploying a covered high-risk AI system. That written check is called a fundamental rights impact assessment.

The European Commission has now drafted a form to make that check easier to do, and on 16 November it asked the public what they think of the form. It is also looking for organisations that will have to fill the form in for real, to try it out. Organisations willing to take part are asked to say so by 4 December 2026. Comments on the form itself are open until 15 January 2027.

Nobody has to use the draft yet. The duty to actually comply starts on 2 December 2027. When the form is finished, organisations covered by the duty will have to use it and tell the market surveillance authority what they found.

One thing worth noticing: the Commission has published the form, not the answer sheet. There is no list of which organisations are covered, no estimate of how many there are, and no mention of what happens to one that skips the check. Those questions are not answered in this announcement, and this article does not answer them either.
