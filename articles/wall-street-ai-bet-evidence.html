Evidence File: AI dependence in U.S. stock markets

This file accompanies the report “Wall Street’s AI Bet: What Happens If the Machines Don’t Deliver?”. It records the primary documents used, the exact sentences relied on, what was verified and by what method, the correction made during editorial review, and what remains unverified.

Verification record

  • Re-tested against live documents on 1 November 2026: every Federal Reserve quotation above, by direct string search of the retrieved pages. All resolved.
  • Re-tested against the IMF Blog text retrieved on 1 November 2026: the patent share, the ETF turnover comparison, the 15-second price-discovery sentence and the March 2020 herd-selling reading. All resolved.
  • Verified against full-text captures: all IMF Global Financial Stability Report material. Both IMF properties returned HTTP 403 to automated retrieval on 1 November 2026, so this material could not be re-fetched. It is verified against captures made before the block and re-tested against those captures. This is a real limit on this file and is stated in the report as well.
  • Corrected during editorial review — attribution: an earlier draft introduced the patent-share, ETF-turnover and 15-second price-discovery figures as “the Fed’s own numbers.” They are the IMF’s October 2024 analysis. The report now attributes them there. The Federal Reserve’s November 2025 box is credited only with the definition, the speed trade-off and the “established practices” assessment. This was the single most material defect found in review.
  • Corrected during editorial review — hedging: the IMF writes that prices seem to move in the direction of the longer-lasting move. An earlier draft dropped the hedge, which turned an observation into an assertion. Restored.
  • Corrected during editorial review — quotation: an earlier draft quoted “the potential for increased herd-like selling during times of stress”; the source reads “potential for increased herd-like selling during times of stress.” The added article is removed.
  • Corrected during editorial review — citation: the May 2026 “upper range of its historical distribution” sentence sits on the report’s overview page, not the asset-valuations page an earlier draft cited alone. Both pages are now cited, which is also why the equity-premium figure and the valuation sentence appear under different URLs.

Deliberate omissions

  • No percentage for algorithmic-trading share of volume. The commonly repeated 60 to 70 per cent figure could not be traced to any primary source. Commercial trackers cite each other; the one authoritative candidate, the SEC’s 2020 Staff Report on Algorithmic Trading, was returned as a PDF whose text could not be extracted in this run. The report states the figure is unsourceable rather than printing it as fact.
  • No percentage for index concentration. Published trackers cluster in the high thirties to low forties for the top-10 share of the S&P 500 but disagree with one another and cite one another. No index-provider or regulator figure was retrieved. The report describes concentration qualitatively.
  • No dot-com valuation-multiple comparison table. No matched dataset was obtained, so the comparison in the report is qualitative and rests on the earnings divergence that the IMF documents.

Stated limits carried into the report

  • The report does not forecast a crash, a date or a magnitude, and says so in its closing section.
  • Two institutional sources (the IMF and the Federal Reserve) are used; there is no independent replication of either body’s analysis and no academic source was introduced.
  • The IMF’s scenario figures are model outputs under stated assumptions, not predictions.
  • Two document sets are age-disclosed in the copy: the IMF report is 18 days old at publication, the Federal Reserve May 2026 data are six months old, and the IMF blog analysis is two years old.

Duplication check

No published article on the site covers AI dependence in equity markets, market concentration, algorithmic trading or the IMF’s financial-stability findings. The nearest published items are a report on Anthropic’s IPO filing and one on European AI investment; neither overlaps this subject matter, and both are linked as related coverage rather than reused.

Provenance of this package

The dossier was researched and drafted with a mechanical verification harness that re-tests every quotation and figure against the retrieved source text. That harness was re-run for this editorial review and returned 44 of 44 assertions held. Its limitation is that it verifies text against captures, not live pages; the live re-testing of the Federal Reserve and IMF Blog material in this file was performed separately, by direct retrieval on 1 November 2026. Nothing in this package has been published, pushed to the production branch or deployed.
