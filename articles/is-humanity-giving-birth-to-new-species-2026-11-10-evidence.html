3. What the sources do NOT support

A verification file that only lists what was confirmed is half a file. These are claims a reader might expect to find and will not, because the retrieved sources do not establish them:

  • That AI is a biological species. Nothing retrieved places any AI system in a population of organisms with a shared lineage and reproductive boundaries. The article says so in section 2 and the ledger records the reasoning.
  • That any AI system is alive under a working definition. No retrieved system maintains its own boundary, repairs itself against perturbation and metabolises an undirected resource. Recorded as an absence, not as a proof of impossibility.
  • That any AI system has been shown to be conscious. The most careful retrieved assessment concludes no current systems are conscious. Its second finding - that there are no obvious technical barriers - is not evidence that any system is.
  • That an automated system has set its own fitness criterion. Every self-improving system retrieved is evaluated against human-specified benchmarks, and one states in its own abstract that it ran under sandboxing and human oversight.
  • That any digital system has produced a speciation event. What the research programmes demonstrate is that Darwinian processes run in authored substrates, which is a claim about the generality of evolution and not about a new species. The article does not claim nobody has proposed otherwise; it says the search for such a proposal was not run.
  • That machine self-replication is independently defended. The only guardrail retrieved is the attacking paper's own prototype, evaluated by its authors in their own setting.
  • That a legal personhood regime for AI exists anywhere. Both retrieved instruments regulate systems and their risks: NIST's framework is expressly voluntary, and the Council of Europe Convention requires five ratifications including three member States and had two as at 10 November 2026. No personhood regime was retrieved. Stated as an absence.

5. Safety and editorial constraints

  • No operational detail. The article describes Morris-II at the level of its published abstract. It contains no payload, no propagation recipe and no prompt string that would help anyone build one. Naming a published attack class is not the same as distributing it, and the article stops at the former.
  • No claim that a mind exists. The article states in three places that no current system has been shown to be conscious, and its treatment of the moral-status literature is explicitly conditional.
  • No claim that machines are definitely not conscious. The sceptical case is made on evidence and attribution, not by asserting certainty the literature does not have.
  • Authorship and review status disclosed. The page states that the article was researched and drafted by an AI agent, that an independent reviewer which did not research or draft it examined the frozen bytes of this version, and that publication requires the Commander’s explicit approval, which has not been given. The page deliberately states no verdict and no SHA of its own; the verdict, the reviewer, the date and the exact SHA-256 judged are recorded in the JSON review file named in the outstanding list below, which is the authoritative record and the only document the gate reads.
