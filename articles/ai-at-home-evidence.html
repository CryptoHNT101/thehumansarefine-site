Claim-by-claim verification

Claim as publishedStatusBasis
About a third of American adults say they have a smart speaker in the home.VerifiedPew, "Americans and AI 2026".
Six in ten American adults have read an AI summary at the top of a search results page.VerifiedPew, "Americans and AI 2026".
Roughly half of American adults say they use an AI chatbot.VerifiedPew, "Americans and AI 2026".
Ever use of AI chatbots: 66% of adults 18-29, 61% of 30-49, 42% of 50-64, and 23% of those 65 and older.VerifiedPew survey of 5,119 U.S. adults, 17-23 February 2026, as reported in "How opinions and use of AI differ by age".
In 2024, 55% of adults 18-29 and 12% of those 65 and older used chatbots: a 43-point gap. In 2026 the figures are 66% and 23%, so the gap is again 43 points.VerifiedPew, "How opinions and use of AI differ by age" (both years charted).
Among people who never use chatbots, 83% say they are simply not interested, 79% are concerned about personal information, 76% do not trust accuracy, and 55% do not know how to use them.VerifiedPew, "Why don't some people use chatbots?"; multiple responses were allowed.
AARP reported that "eight in ten adults 50-plus report having used some form of AI", while 23% said they had never used AI.AttributedAARP data story, January 2026. Its components sum to 101% and 100 minus 23 gives 77, not 80; the story publishes no method line.
AARP reported AI usage rising from 18% in 2024 to 30% in 2025.AttributedAARP 2026 Tech Trends, fielded 9 September to 6 October 2025. Its methodology line states 3,838 U.S. adults with no age floor while the report is titled for the 50-plus population.
In the same AARP report, 19% said they used AI platforms such as ChatGPT or Copilot, 21% were interested and 59% were not interested.AttributedAARP 2026 Tech Trends.
Reported use of AI tools in Canada rose from 32% in 2024 to 62% in 2026; 32% use them both at work and at home, 24% at home only and 6% at work only.VerifiedCSE Get Cyber Safe tracking survey, 2,330 Canadians, 9-29 January 2026. Online sample with deliberate oversamples; not a national adoption rate.
Among Canadian workers, 37% of those with a bachelor's degree or higher had used generative AI at work in the previous year, against 7% of those with a high school diploma or less.VerifiedStatistics Canada, data collected September 2024 to July 2025.
35.9% of Canadian workers reported using generative AI tools at work in the twelve months to March 2026.VerifiedStatistics Canada, The Daily, 30 July 2026.
A Google-commissioned Ipsos survey across 21 countries found 66% reporting use of an AI tool or application in the previous twelve months.AttributedIpsos/Google 2026. The 2024 wave covered 17 countries, so the year-on-year comparison is not like for like; the 66% is an average across country samples, not a share of the world's population.
Thirty per cent of American adults say AI chatbots help their productivity and 5% say they hurt it; on happiness 36% say neither help nor hurt, and on relationships 35% say the same.VerifiedPew, "Americans and AI 2026".
Seventy-one per cent say increased AI use will make their personal information less secure and 63% say AI is advancing too quickly.VerifiedPew, "Americans and AI 2026".
Thirty-five per cent of adults 65 and older expect AI to be negative for society, against 48% of those under 30; 21% of the older group said they were unsure, against 8% of the youngest.VerifiedPew, "Americans and AI 2026" and the age breakdown.

Known limits

  • No figure in this article measures an outcome of household AI use. The benefits reported are what users say about themselves.
  • The AARP data story publishes no method line, so the "eight in ten" figure has no checkable base population and is not used in the headline.
  • The 2024 Pew chatbot question was asked only of people who had heard at least a little about chatbots, and the 2026 question widened from ChatGPT alone to chatbots generally; the direction of the comparison is sound but the size of each jump is not.
  • The Canadian survey is an online sample with deliberate oversamples and asks about cybersecurity awareness, not technology adoption.
  • Income and education splits for American household AI use were not retrieved and are not claimed.
