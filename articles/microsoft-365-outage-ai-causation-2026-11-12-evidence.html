Known limits

  • The article's timeline compresses several advisory updates into single sentences, and the 20:38 UTC sentence also carries the 20:56 and 23:59 material. The per-update wording, in Microsoft's own sequence, is held in the saved capture rather than reproduced sentence by sentence in the article.
  • A raw text extraction of the mirror can truncate a long paragraph mid-sentence (the 01:06 UTC paragraph is the one affected, cut at "recent network con"). The full clause is present in the page as retrieved and in the hash-recorded capture of it, and quotations in this package were tested by the newsroom's quotation verifier against the page as retrieved, not against a truncated extract.
  • The mirror's own header carries a generated-at stamp that lags the newest advisory content it prints. That is an inconsistency in the mirror, reported here rather than reconciled, and it is why retrieval timestamps in this file are the times this newsroom actually read the page.
  • Primary source is a third-party mirror of Microsoft's admin-centre advisories, refreshed every 15 minutes and retained for 30 days. That retention is why the article is dated by its last read rather than by its first: a quotation from an update the mirror has already rolled past cannot be re-verified against it. Quotations were hash-recorded at retrieval on 12 November 2026 again at 03:15 UTC on 13 November 2026 for the updates from 00:45 to 02:58 UTC, and again at 04:22 UTC for the 03:34 and 03:36 UTC updates. Once the mirror rolls past 30 days, re-verification requires Microsoft's own service health record or the post-incident report. A capture of the retrieved page is preserved for re-verification.
